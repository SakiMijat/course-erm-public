> *"On this position, what characterizes science is not that the methods employed are unique to science, but that the methods are more carefully employed... the greater care in detecting and eliminating sources of error."*
>
> — **H. Andersen & B. Hepburn**, summarizing Paul Hoyningen-Huene's *Systematicity: The Nature of Science* (2013), in *"Scientific Method"*, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*.

---

Our working definition: **Empirical science** is the construction of parsimonious, falsifiable models tested against physical observation, with the permanent understanding that all conclusions are provisional.

---

### The 4 Epistemic Pillars

Epistemology studies how raw observations become valid, reliable knowledge. The four pillars separate genuine empirical science from superstition, sophisticated pattern-matching, and self-sealing dogma:

1. **Empiricism:** Physical observation is essential for filtering which models describe our reality and which are purely theoretical possibilities. *Lecture Example: The 3 geometries (Flat, Spherical, Hyperbolic).*
2. **Parsimony:** Across study design and modeling, we adopt the minimum necessary assumptions, parameters, and unobservable entities, placing the burden of proof on added complexity. *Lecture Example: Einstein’s Special Relativity over Lorentz’s Aether Theory; Ptolemy's epicycles vs. Copernicus.*
3. **Falsifiability:** For a theory to be scientific, it must make precise, risky predictions in advance that can inherently be proven false by an observable finding. *Lecture Example: Louis Pasteur’s 1881 Pouilly-le-Fort anthrax trial vs. Freudian psychoanalysis.*
4. **Fallibilism:** Absolute certainty is impossible in empirical science. All scientific findings, models, and theories are provisional and permanently open to revision. *Lecture Example: Decades of medical consensus that stress and acid cause ulcers.*

---

### The 4 Tiers of Fallibility

When an empirical claim fails, it breaks at one or more of four distinct levels:

1. **Tier 1: Theoretical Failure:** A failure in the conceptual, causal, or axiomatic framework used to interpret reality. The mental model is structurally wrong, blinding the researcher regardless of methodology and measurement quality. *Lecture Example: Astronomers spending 50 years hunting the ghost planet Vulcan; the dogma that pH 1.5 acid makes life impossible.*
2. **Tier 2: Methodological Failure:** A failure in study design, sampling protocol, operationalization, or experimental controls. *Lecture Example: Hospital labs routinely discarding bacterial cultures at 48 hours; Moniz's uncontrolled 1949 lobotomy study.*
3. **Tier 3: Instrumental Failure:** A failure in the physical measuring instrument, sensor, or chemical assay that distorts, corrupts, or is blind to the physical signal present. *Lecture Example: Dr. Palmer using standard Gram/H&E tissue stains; the 2011 OPERA loose fiber-optic cable producing faster-than-light neutrinos.*
4. **Tier 4: Stochastic Failure:** A failure caused purely by random noise (thermodynamical, biological, environmental, and, most commonly, **finite sample variability**), when nobody made a mistake. *Lecture Example: The 2015 CERN 750 GeV bump.*
